Role-based learning pathway

One process, three perspectives, observable practice.

Independently reconstructed fictional example · Created for Ari Damasco’s portfolio. Harbor Community Services is an invented organization. All people, policies, amounts, and scenarios are fictional. This is not an employer document or a product configuration guide.

01 / A shared scenario

Harbor Community Services needs $750 in workshop supplies. The fictional program manager may approve up to $1,000; the finance director reviews larger amounts. No self-approval is permitted. Use a training environment with fictional records.

Common foundation: Purpose → required information → approval authority → status → support route.

Path 01 / Requester

Create a complete request and keep it moving.

  1. Understand the route. Identify the information an approver needs and who reviews it.
  2. Build & submit. Practice a fictional $750 supply request with a purpose, coding, and supporting document.
  3. Track & correct. Find the request’s status, respond to a return, and resubmit with a clear correction.

Facilitator check: Submit one complete practice request and explain how to handle a return.

A request is returned because its purpose is unclear. What should you do?

Clarify the business purpose, make the requested correction, and resubmit through the agreed workflow. Do not create a duplicate request.

Path 02 / Approver

Make a defensible decision within your authority.

  1. Know your authority. Read the fictional approval matrix and identify your spending limit.
  2. Review with purpose. Check business purpose, coding, support, and whether the request belongs in your queue.
  3. Decide & explain. Approve a compliant request, return an incomplete one, and escalate one outside your authority.

Facilitator check: Explain three decisions: approve, return for correction, and escalate.

Your fictional limit is $1,000. A complete $1,250 request arrives. Approve it?

No. Follow the organization’s escalation route to the authorized reviewer. Complete documentation does not override an approval limit.

Path 03 / Administrator

Maintain a workflow that reflects approved policy.

  1. Map policy to roles. Translate a fictional approval matrix into roles and routing requirements.
  2. Configure & validate. In a training environment, test access and routing using $750 and $1,250 requests.
  3. Maintain & support. Document a policy change, retest affected routes, and identify when to escalate an issue.

Facilitator check: Demonstrate an authorized route, an exception, and an access check; record the results.

An approver changes roles. Is changing their account enough?

Review affected access and approval routes, apply the approved change, test the relevant scenarios, and document the update before relying on the revised workflow.

Transfer to the job

Provide a concise role guide and a named internal support contact. If a learner cannot explain the decision or complete the practice task, revisit that step and retry before independent use. Adapt policies and examples to the approved customer workflow.
